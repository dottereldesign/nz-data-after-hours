Fresh Leads

1. Pirates or sandworms? — Which production got the bigger NZ rebate?

Put Dune: Part Two beside season two of Our Flag Means Death. Pick the one you think got the bigger New Zealand screen rebate. The Film Commission approved NZ$367,419 for Dune and NZ$17.93 million for the pirate comedy. Reveal the qualifying local spend alongside each answer: NZ$1.84 million and NZ$71.70 million respectively. Suddenly the production you thought was bigger depends on where you’re standing.

Combine the domestic and international approvals into a title-by-title guessing game. Compare final rebates with final rebates, open the original approval, then send a friend the same two titles with the amounts hidden. International approvals · Domestic approvals

2. How many complaints sink a pizza ad? — One advertiser, two very different verdicts

Hell Pizza drew 685 complaints after mailing condoms to promote a pizza. The complaint was upheld. An Easter billboard about hot cross buns drew 178 complaints and stayed on the right side of the board’s ruling. Which campaign would you have pulled?

Give each ad a dot. Plot the number of complaints and colour it by the decision, with petitions counted separately. Click one brand to follow it across the Advertising Standards Authority and the Commerce Commission. Hell’s later plant-based Burger Pizza landed in a different file altogether: a warning over how the topping was described. ASA complaint counts · Ad rulings · Burger Pizza warning

3. How much did it cost to come second? — The receipts from election night

In the 2023 judicial recounts, Tāmaki Makaurau’s margin went from four votes to 42. Mt Albert’s went from 20 to 18. Now put the candidates’ expense returns next to those numbers. Which losing campaign would you guess spent the most in a seat decided by fewer than 100 votes?

Match each candidate to the final result by name, electorate and election. Give each close race two spending bars and a switch between the official count and the recount where one took place. A reader can see how much each side declared and exactly how close the finish was. Candidate returns · Recount results, table 7

4. Who actually paid full price? — The sale that barely ever ended

Imagine a wall of nearly 6,000 bike receipts. Tap the ones you think were full price. In the sales the Commerce Commission analysed at Bike Barn, only 30 bikes sold at the claimed full price. The joint operators were fined $800,000.

Build a shelf of real discount cases. For each item, reveal the advertised price, what investigators could establish about past sales, and the eventual outcome. A second shelf could follow handbags: the Strandbags case includes products that had never sold at the advertised higher price. Bike Barn findings · Strandbags findings

5. Who voted with their political opposite? — An unlikely-pairs guide to conscience votes

Parliament voted 77–44 at the final reading on marriage equality in 2013 and 69–51 on assisted dying in 2019. Pick two MPs who served for both votes. Before revealing the roll calls, guess whether they ended up on the same side twice, once or never.

Join the two named divisions by MP, keeping absences and proxy votes visible. Let readers open a bill and see which MPs from different parties shared their choice. Every comparison links to the roll call, so the argument in the group chat can start with what the MPs actually did. Marriage equality roll call · End of Life Choice roll call
